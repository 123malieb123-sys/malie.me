03 / Request procedure

The PAIA Guide and making a request

The official PAIA Guide

The Information Regulator provides a Guide under section 10 of PAIA to explain rights of access, the request procedure, available forms, possible fees and remedies. It is available from the Information Regulator's PAIA page, with language and accessible-format options through the Regulator.

How to request a record

A requester should use the prescribed Form 2 — Request for Access to Record (available through the Information Regulator's PAIA forms page), specify the record sought, explain the right to be exercised or protected, and submit the completed form to hello@malie.me or the business address. Information reasonably needed to identify the requester and the record may be requested.

Requests are considered under PAIA, ordinarily within 30 days, subject to any lawful extension. Access is not automatic: statutory grounds for refusal may apply, including the privacy of another person and protected confidential or commercially sensitive records. Decisions will be communicated through the prescribed process.

Fees and remedies

The Regulator's published March 2026 private-body fees include a R140 request fee where applicable, and R2 per A4 printed page; statutory exemptions and other prescribed access, search or reproduction fees apply. A requester will be informed of applicable fees before payment. Personal requesters and exempt categories are treated according to the current law and fee schedule.

Where a request is refused, the requester may use the complaint process of the Information Regulator or the courts as provided for in PAIA. There is no ordinary internal appeal procedure for a private body. The current official forms and fees are linked in section 09.

07 / Personal information

Processing of personal information

7.1 Purposes of processing

The business may process information to respond to enquiries, arrange consultations, assess programme suitability, deliver agreed English coaching, communicate about lessons, administer invoicing and payments where relevant, maintain essential business records, and meet applicable legal obligations.

7.2 Data subjects and information categories

Data subjectsInformation categories
Prospective clients / enquirersNames, email addresses, country or time zone, programme interests, communication goals and enquiry content
Consultation bookersNames, email addresses, booking times and relevant booking responses submitted to TidyCal
Coaching clients, where enrolledContact and scheduling details, agreed services, communication goals, teaching feedback and any billing records generated
Business service providersBusiness contact and contractual details where service-provider relationships exist

Clients should not submit patient-identifiable details or unnecessary sensitive personal information through ordinary website enquiry or booking channels.

7.3 Recipients and service providers

Personal information may be shared only as needed with the business owner, email and scheduling service providers (including the business's Gmail/forwarding infrastructure and TidyCal), providers used to conduct online coaching, and professional advisers or authorities where lawfully required. Online coaching service providers may change over time; relevant supplier details are maintained as part of the business's records.

7.4 Transborder processing

The website uses external services, including TidyCal for bookings, Google-related email services and GitHub Pages for hosting; service-provider infrastructure may involve processing outside South Africa. Any cross-border transfer of personal information must satisfy the conditions of section 72 of POPIA. Hosting and service-provider arrangements are subject to review as the business develops.

7.5 Information security safeguards

The business is required to maintain appropriate technical and organisational safeguards, including access control, secure accounts, device protection, restricted sharing, retention/deletion procedures, and a process to respond to potential security compromises. The business must review and maintain safeguards appropriate to its actual information-processing practices. This manual does not certify that every safeguard example listed has been implemented.
